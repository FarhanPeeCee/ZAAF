## Summary

Zaaf is not a madrassa. It is the one-to-one tuition that keeps a Samastha madrassa student up with her class, and the whole site should say so in its first line. That single sentence settles the positioning, removes any suggestion of competing with the madrassa, and is far easier to sell to a parent than asking her to choose an alternative.

Build a nine-page showcase site that stores nothing and sends every enquiry to WhatsApp.

**Primary audience:** parents of children enrolled in a Samastha madrassa — in Kerala, and in the Gulf through the board's online programme — whose child needs help keeping pace, or is facing a board exam. **The one action:** open WhatsApp.

**Who Zaaf teaches:** girls and women, and boys up to Class 4. Women-led, with women teachers. Say this plainly on the site; it saves the wrong enquiries and reassures the right ones.

**The product is parental control.** The parent chooses the days, how many a week, the timing, and whether the class is taught in Malayalam or English. For a family on Gulf working hours, across the time difference from Kerala, that flexibility is the offer. The fee follows from those choices rather than sitting in a fixed table.

**Success is measured by** WhatsApp opens per week, by the page they came from and by device. With no forms and nothing stored, that outbound tap is the only conversion event the site can record.

**Scope:** Home, About Zaaf, Features, Classes, Teachers, Reviews, Fees, Contact, and Become a Teacher. No forms, no accounts, no payments, no database.

### The four decisions that shape everything else

1. **Lead with support, not with madrassa.** The first line should read as tuition for madrassa students. A parent already paying for a madrassa is not shopping for another one; she wants help with the one she has.
2. **Let her choose her board, then her class.** Zaaf teaches both the SKIMVB and the SKSVB syllabus. A parent is loyal to one and will scan for it before anything else, so the board has to be visible on the home page rather than buried in a FAQ. Covering both is unusual and worth saying out loud.
3. **Make the exam years loud.** Zaaf prepares students for the board exams at Classes 5, 7, 10 and 12. That is the most urgent reason anyone buys tuition, it recurs every year, and it deserves its own page plus prominence on those four class pages.
4. **State who is taught, in plain words.** Girls and women at every class, boys up to Class 4, taught by women. Putting the boundary on the site beats discovering it in a WhatsApp conversation.

### Biggest risks

WhatsApp is the entire funnel, so reply speed is the business rather than a detail; with nothing stored, an unanswered message is an invisible lost customer. Separately, **naming SKIMVB and SKSVB on a commercial site carries an affiliation question** — if Zaaf is independent of both, the site must say so clearly. See section 11.

### What is needed next

1. The fee basis: rate per session by language, and how days per week changes it.
2. Teacher details, one row each, and who consents to a photograph.
3. Six to ten reviews from current families, with permission and agreed attribution.
4. Confirmation of whether Zaaf prepares students for the board exams at Classes 5, 7, 10 and 12.
5. The WhatsApp business number, who answers it, and during which hours.
6. Logo and brand assets, or a decision to commission an identity.

## 1. Context, goals and constraints

The site is a showcase, and WhatsApp is the funnel. That single decision settles most of what follows: no forms, no accounts, no payments, no database, and one measurable event. It also concentrates the risk, because a site that stores nothing cannot recover an enquiry that nobody answers.

### What Zaaf is

A women-led tuition service from Kerala that supports children already studying in the Samastha madrassa system. Those children work through Classes 1 to 12 against a set syllabus, with board exams along the way. Zaaf teaches them one-to-one online, alongside their madrassa rather than instead of it. Girls and women at any class, and boys up to Class 4. The parent chooses the days, how many a week, the timing, and whether the class is taught in Malayalam or English.

### Business goals and how to observe them

With no forms and nothing stored, the site itself can observe exactly one thing. Everything past the tap has to be counted by hand, so decide now who keeps that tally and make it a weekly habit rather than an afterthought.

| Goal | Observable measure | Where it is measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turn visitors into conversations | WhatsApp opens per week, by page and device | The site, as an outbound-click event |
| Find out which page persuades | Share of opens starting from Classes, Teachers, Fees or Reviews | The site |
| Reach Gulf families | Visits from Gulf countries | The site |
| Catch exam-season demand | Visits and opens on the Class 5, 7, 10 and 12 pages | The site |
| Turn conversations into trial classes | Chats started, then trial classes held | By hand, from WhatsApp |
| Turn trials into students | Trials that became paying students | By hand |
| Keep teachers supplied | Teacher enquiries per month | By hand, from WhatsApp |

The first three are free once analytics is set up. The last three need a person and a spreadsheet, and without them Zaaf will never know whether the site works.

### Scope at launch

Nine pages, built from a handful of templates, with teachers, classes and reviews as repeating content. No forms, no logins, no payment handling, no scheduling tool. The teacher application is a WhatsApp conversation like every other enquiry.

### Constraints

- **Gulf mobile first.** Most visitors will be on a phone, many on a shared family device. Page weight and load speed matter more than anything decorative.
- **A time difference to design around.** Kerala runs ahead of the Gulf by an hour and a half to two and a half hours depending on the country. Any time shown on the site needs its timezone named, or it will be read wrongly.
- **Two scripts.** Malayalam for parents who prefer it, English for those who do not, plus Arabic terms throughout. The type system has to carry all three without looking improvised.
- **No stored data means no safety net.** There is no record of a missed enquiry, no abandoned-form recovery, no list to follow up. Response time on WhatsApp is the whole business.
- **No in-house developer.** Teachers, classes, fees and reviews must be editable by a non-technical person, or the site goes stale within a year.
- **Content is the critical path.** The build is short. Real teacher profiles, real reviews with permission, and real class-by-class detail are what will hold it up.

## 2. Audiences and top tasks

Every visitor already has a madrassa. That single fact shapes all the copy: nobody arriving here needs persuading that religious schooling matters, or that online teaching works. They are looking for help with something already underway, usually because something specific has gone wrong — a child is behind, or an exam is coming.

|  | The Gulf parent | The exam-year parent | The child |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Where | UAE, Saudi, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain | Anywhere, including Kerala | With her parents |
| Situation | Her child studies at the online madrassa from abroad, with no teacher in the room and nobody at home who can help with the syllabus | Her child is in Class 5, 7, 10 or 12 with a board exam ahead | Classes 1 to 12. Girls to any class, boys to Class 4 |
| Comes to find | Someone who knows this exact syllabus and will sit with her child one-to-one | Focused help before the exam, starting soon | Who the teacher is, and whether this will be bearable |
| Worries about | Whether a tutor really knows the Samastha syllabus; fitting classes around Gulf working hours | Leaving it too late; paying for help that does not target the exam | Another hour of class after school |
| Decided by | Does the class level match, and can she choose the timing | Does Zaaf know the exam, and can it start this week | Not her decision, but she can make it fail |
| Needs to see | Classes 1 to 12 laid out, the language choice, flexible timings, women teachers | The exam classes named explicitly, and a fast route to a conversation | A teacher who sounds like a person |

### Why supporting rather than replacing is the easier sale

A family already enrolled in a madrassa has accepted the format, has a timetable, and knows exactly where their child struggles. Zaaf does not have to win an argument about whether online religious education works, only about whether this particular help is worth paying for. That is a much shorter conversation, and the site should take advantage of it by naming the situation in the first line instead of introducing itself as an academy.

### The exam years are the sharpest opportunity

Board exams at Classes 5, 7, 10 and 12 create urgency that nothing else on this site can manufacture, and they come round every year. If Zaaf prepares students for them, those four class pages carry the strongest message, the most search traffic and the shortest path to a decision. Treat them as the four most important pages after the home page.

### Ranked top tasks

These drive the navigation. The first four must be reachable in one tap from the home page on a phone.

1. **Start a WhatsApp conversation.** The only action the site has. On every page and in a fixed mobile bar.
2. **Find what is taught at my child's class.** The structure parents already think in.
3. **Check that Zaaf knows the syllabus and the exams.** The question behind every other question.
4. **See who the teachers are.** Names, qualifications, languages, classes taken.
5. **Understand what it costs and how the cost is worked out.**
6. **Check the timings can work** across the time difference.
7. **Read what other families say.** Reviews from Gulf parents outweigh any claim Zaaf makes about itself.
8. Apply to teach.

Tasks 1 to 7 shape the navigation. Task 8 lives in the footer with a page of its own, and never competes with the parent-facing journey.

### What must not drive the navigation

Zaaf's own internal structure, and religious category headings that assume knowledge the visitor may not have. Avoid anything that reads as a rival madrassa: a parent who thinks she is being asked to switch will leave. The words **tuition** and **support** should reach her before the word **academy** does.

## 3. Peer scan

Zaaf's competition is not the madrassa. It is the relative who tries to help with the syllabus, the tutor a family finds through someone at the mosque, and the general online tutors who teach school subjects but not this. None of those is organised, findable or specialised, which is why a site that is clearly and specifically madrassa tuition has room to win.

**Method and its limit.** This is a desk scan from search listings. ### The most important finding

The board's own online programme is reportedly open only to **families with no recognised madrassa nearby**, with entry at age five and higher levels requiring a pass from a recognised madrassa. If that is accurate, it describes Zaaf's customers exactly: the Gulf families enrolled in it are, by definition, the ones with no teacher in the room and nobody at home who knows the syllabus. The programme does not compete with Zaaf; it manufactures Zaaf's market and grows it every year.

The scan also indicates public exams at **Classes 5, 7, 10 and 12**. If confirmed, that is an annual, predictable surge in exactly the kind of urgent demand one-to-one tuition is bought for. **Verify both points against [samastha.in](https://samastha.in) before publishing anything that refers to them,** and see section 11 on whether the site may name the board at all.

### Three gaps worth taking

1. **Nobody positions as madrassa tuition.** The market splits into madrassas and general tutors, with nothing in between that a parent can search for and find. Being unambiguously the support service for madrassa students is an unoccupied position, and it is simply what Zaaf already does.
2. **Nobody organises by class.** Qur'an academies sell courses; tutors sell subjects. Main navigation running Class 1 to Class 12, with the exam years marked, is instantly legible to these parents and cannot be imitated by a generic provider.
3. **Nobody explains the fee.** Across every group, prices are absent or buried. Zaaf's fee varies with days, timing and language, which is a reason to explain the basis clearly rather than to hide it.

### What not to copy

The general Qur'an academies all run the same template: stock photography, badge clutter, round-the-clock availability claims and an anonymous pool of certified teachers. That look signals scale. Zaaf's advantage is the opposite impression — a specific syllabus, named teachers, a parent who chooses — so the site should feel nothing like them.

## 4. Information architecture

Nine pages plus two repeating templates. The Classes section is the spine: it is the structure parents already carry in their heads, and it is the one thing a general tutoring site cannot copy.

### Sitemap

- **Classes** `/classes`
  - Class detail `/classes/class-1` through `/classes/class-12` — one template, twelve pages, each covering both boards
- **SKIMVB syllabus** `/syllabus/skimvb`
- **SKSVB syllabus** `/syllabus/sksvb`
- **Exam preparation** `/exams` — Classes 5, 7, 10 and 12, both boards
- **Teachers** `/teachers`
  - Teacher profile `/teachers/<name>` — one template
- **Fees** `/fees`
- **Reviews** `/reviews`
- **How it works** `/features`
- **About Zaaf** `/about`
- **Contact** `/contact`
- **Become a Teacher** `/teach`
- **Privacy** `/privacy`

Three notes on naming. Label the features page **How it works** in the navigation even though the slug stays `/features`: a parent is not shopping for features, she wants to know how a class actually runs. Use `/fees`, not `/pricing`, because fees is the word a madrassa family uses. And keep the board abbreviations — **SKIMVB** and **SKSVB** — exactly as parents write them, since those are the terms they will search for and scan for.

### Two boards: the main structural decision on this site

Zaaf teaches both the SKIMVB and the SKSVB syllabus, which gives the site a second axis alongside class. A parent is attached to one board and will look for it before she looks at anything else, so the site has to answer *is my board covered* within the first screen. Getting this wrong is the difference between a site that feels made for her and one that feels generic.

There are two sensible structures.

**Recommended: twelve class pages that each cover both boards, plus one landing page per board.** The class page is where the parent lands from a search for her child's class, and showing both syllabi there — clearly separated, her board selectable — keeps the content in one place and avoids twenty-four pages that would inevitably go thin. The two board pages then exist to capture board-name searches and to say plainly that Zaaf covers that syllabus, linking into the twelve classes. Fourteen pages in total.

**Alternative: twenty-four class pages, one per board per class.** Better for search, because each page targets an exact query, and cleaner for the parent who never sees the other board's material. The cost is double the content to write and maintain, and a real risk of twenty-four pages that each say very little. Choose this only if the syllabi differ substantially enough to justify it, which is a question only Zaaf can answer.

Either way, the home page needs a visible board chooser, and every WhatsApp message should carry the board as well as the class.

### Do the twelve class pages need to exist at launch?

Yes, but they can be short. Twelve thin accurate pages beat one long page listing everything: a parent lands directly on her child's class from search, each page carries its own WhatsApp button already naming the class and board, and the set demonstrates that Zaaf really follows the syllabus rather than claiming to. If detail for all twelve is not ready, launch with the classes that can be described properly — starting with 5, 7, 10 and 12 — and add the rest.

**Draft hero, option A.** *Madrassa tuition, at your timings.* — *Zaaf Academy gives one-to-one support to Samastha madrassa students, online, in Malayalam or English. You choose the days, the timings and how many classes a week.*

### 02–03 · Classes `/classes` and class detail

**Purpose.** Prove that Zaaf teaches the real syllabus, and give each class its own entry point from search.

The listing explains the class system in two sentences for parents who did not grow up with it, puts the board choice at the top, then shows Classes 1 to 12 as a grid with the typical age beside each and the exam years marked. Each class page carries: the age it suits, what is covered that year **in each board's syllabus, clearly separated**, roughly how it divides across the year, what the child should already know, which teachers take it, how many days a week is usual, and whether a board exam falls at the end of it.

**Two syllabus pages,** `/syllabus/skimvb` and `/syllabus/sksvb`, each saying plainly that Zaaf covers that board, what the progression looks like across the twelve classes, which exams fall where, and which teachers know it. These exist mainly to be found: a parent searching her board's name should land on a page that answers *yes, we teach yours* without her having to read around it.

**The exam-preparation page** is the most urgent page on the site. It should name both boards' exams separately, cover Classes 5, 7, 10 and 12, say how far ahead a family should start, what preparation involves, and how quickly Zaaf can begin. **Do not merge the two boards' exam schedules**; if they differ, showing one set of dates to the wrong parent is the kind of error this audience will not forgive.

**WhatsApp messages, carrying the board as well as the class:** from a class page, `Hi Zaaf, I am asking about Class 5 classes for my child. We follow the SKIMVB syllabus.` From the exam page, `Hi Zaaf, my child has the Class 10 exam coming up and needs help.`

**SEO.** One title per class, for example `Class 5 Madrassa Syllabus Online | Zaaf Academy`, plus board-specific titles on the two syllabus pages and the exam page. Board names are high-intent search terms that almost nobody is competing for, and together with the class pages they are where this site will earn its organic traffic.

**Risk.** Twelve near-identical pages with no real content would be worse than four good ones. Launch with the classes that can be described properly.

### 04–05 · Teachers `/teachers` and profiles

**Purpose.** Convert. A parent handing her child to a stranger online wants to see a person, not a pool.

The listing shows each teacher with a photo or a designed avatar, name, which classes she takes, languages taught, and years of experience. Profiles add a short bio in her own voice, her qualifications, where she studied, which syllabus she knows, and a WhatsApp button naming her.

**Design note that cannot be an afterthought.** Some teachers will decline a photograph. Design the no-photo state first so it looks deliberate beside a real portrait, rather than broken.

**WhatsApp message:** `Hi Zaaf, I would like to ask about classes with [teacher name].`

**Risk.** Publishing a name or photograph without written consent.

### 06 · Fees `/fees`

**Purpose.** Remove price as an unknown without pretending the fee is fixed. It depends on session length, days per week and language, so the page explains the basis and shows worked examples.

Structure: one line on what the fee depends on; a rate table by language and session length; two or three worked examples such as *two days a week in Malayalam* and *four days a week in English*; what is included; how payment is made and in which currency; what happens to a missed class; and whether there is a free or discounted first class.

`[CONTENT NEEDED: rates by language and session length, currency, payment method — owner: Founder]`

**WhatsApp message:** `Hi Zaaf, I would like to know the fee for three days a week in Malayalam.`

**Risk.** Quoting rupees to a parent who thinks in dirhams, with no indication of which applies. Decide whether to show both.

### 07 · Reviews `/reviews`

**Purpose.** Carry the persuasion the academy cannot do for itself. For this audience a review from a Malayali family in Sharjah is worth more than any claim on the home page.

Each review needs the parent's name as permitted, the country, the child's class, and how long they have studied. Country and class matter: a parent in Qatar scanning for someone in her situation should find one. Group them by country if there are enough.

`[CONTENT NEEDED: six to ten reviews with written permission and agreed attribution — owner: Founder]`

**Risk.** Anonymous, undated praise reads as invented. First name, country and class is the minimum that persuades.

### 08 · How it works `/features`

**Purpose.** Answer the practical questions in one place: what happens in a class, what the child needs, how the timetable is agreed, what happens when a class is missed, how progress is reported to the parent, and how the time difference is handled.

State the timezone explicitly wherever an hour appears. A parent in Riyadh and a teacher in Kerala are two and a half hours apart, and a time written without its zone will be read wrongly.

### 09–12 · About, Contact, Become a Teacher, Privacy

**About Zaaf** carries why the academy exists, who runs it, the tradition it teaches in, and how teachers are chosen. Write it in the first person; this is the page that convinces a sceptical reader who has already read everything else.

**Contact** leads with WhatsApp, then phone, then email, with reply hours stated in both Indian and Gulf time. Show the number as selectable text as well as a tap link.

**Become a Teacher** states what Zaaf looks for, which classes and languages are needed, and what the commitment is, then sends the application to WhatsApp like everything else. Message: `Hi Zaaf, I would like to apply to teach.`

**Privacy** is short and honest, and worth having even though nothing is stored: say that the site holds no personal data, name the analytics used, and note that messages sent through WhatsApp are handled under WhatsApp's own terms.

## 6. Content model

Five repeating content types, and no stored visitor data of any kind. This is the content the site publishes, not anything it collects. Required fields are **bold**; a record missing one should not be publishable.

| Type | Used on | Fields | Count at launch | Maintained by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Board | Syllabus pages, class pages, home chooser | **Name**, **abbreviation**, **slug**, what its progression looks like, exam classes, teachers who know it | 2 | Teaching lead |
| Class | Classes listing, class pages, home grid | **Number, 1 to 12**, **typical age**, **slug**, **what is covered, per board**, what the child should already know, how the year divides, usual days per week, exam year or not, teachers who take it, published flag | 12 | Teaching lead |
| Teacher | Teachers listing, profiles, home, class pages | **Name**, **slug**, **classes taken**, **boards known**, **languages taught**, **qualification**, **years teaching**, bio in her own words, where she studied, portrait, avatar fallback, **photo-consent flag**, published flag | However many there are | Teaching lead |
| Review | Reviews page, home, class pages | **Quote**, **parent name as permitted**, **country**, **child's class**, **permission on file flag**, board, how long they have studied, date given | 6–10 | Founder |
| Fee rate | Fees page, class pages | **Language**, **session length**, **rate**, **currency**, how days per week changes it, what is included | 4–6 | Founder |
| FAQ | How it works, Fees, class pages | **Question in the parent's words**, **answer**, **topic**, board if it differs, display order | 12–20 | Founder |

### Three fields that carry real consequences

- **`photo-consent` on Teacher.** A portrait must not render unless this is set. Once a photograph is published and indexed it cannot reliably be withdrawn.
- **`permission on file` on Review.** A quote naming a family and their child, published without written permission, is a real problem rather than a discourtesy.
- **`published` flags everywhere.** Half-finished class and teacher records will exist. They must be invisible rather than live and thin.

### The class record is the most valuable thing here

Twelve properly written class records give Zaaf twelve search entry points, twelve WhatsApp buttons that already name a class, and visible proof that the real syllabus is being followed. It is also the hardest content to write, because it has to be accurate. Treat it as the main content task, not as filler around the teacher profiles.

### What is deliberately not modelled

No enquiries, no leads, no bookings, no schedules, no attendance, no payments, no accounts. All of that lives in WhatsApp and in whatever Zaaf keeps privately. The site publishes; it does not record.

### Known gaps

- No class-by-class syllabus detail, which blocks twelve of the twenty-five pages.
- No teacher records, and no consent decisions on photographs.
- No reviews with permission on file.
- No fee rates in any currency.
- No confirmation of which board's syllabus is followed, or whether Zaaf prepares for its exams.

## 7. Media and imagery plan

The two most persuasive images on this site cost nothing to make: a photograph of the actual madrassa textbook for a class, and a screenshot of a real class in progress. A Malayali parent recognises those textbooks instantly, and recognising them answers the question the whole site is trying to answer.

### Principles

- **Every image has one job:** orient, prove, humanise, explain, reassure, or delight. If the job cannot be named, leave it out. This sector overspends on delight and underspends on prove.
- **No stock photography of people.** A licensed photograph of a child at a laptop, placed where a visitor reads her as a Zaaf student, is a misrepresentation. Stock is admissible only for abstract texture.
- **No identifiable students, as the default.** This removes a whole category of consent risk and costs Zaaf nothing it needs.
- **No generic Gulf imagery.** Skylines and date palms say nothing about teaching, and they read as filler to the people who live there.
- **The system must work without faces,** because some teachers will decline a photograph. Textbook pages, notebooks, Malayalam and Arabic script, pattern and texture carry the site; a consenting portrait then counts for more.
- **Key facts never live only inside an image.** A fee, a class number or a timing must exist as text on the page, for search and for screen readers.

### Sources

A studio shoot is not realistic for teachers spread across Kerala. The practical mix is self-captured photographs to a written brief, real screenshots for anything that proves, a small commissioned pattern or avatar set, and nothing licensed except texture.

**Self-shot brief to send each teacher.** Phone camera, rear lens, held vertically. Face a window so the light is on your face, not behind you. Plain wall behind. Frame from mid-chest up with space above the head. Portrait-mode blur off. Send the original file, not a WhatsApp-compressed copy.

## 8. User flows

Three flows, and the first one is the whole business. The drawing below is the most important page in this document, because it shows how little of this journey the website can see.

> **Diagram: parent to first class** — 9 steps, 1 decision, nothing stored.
> The drawing does not survive export to Markdown. View it in the living document linked above.

The website's work ends four steps in, at the tap. Everything below the dashed line happens in a chat, leaves no trace on the site, and is where the money is won or lost. The box marked in red is the real cost of storing nothing: a parent who messages at eleven at night and gets no reply by morning has vanished, and Zaaf will never know she existed.

### Flow 1 · Parent to first class

**Trigger:** any page. **Entry points:** home, any class page, any teacher profile, Fees, Reviews, the fixed mobile bar.

| Step | What the site must do | Where it breaks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrives | Say in one screen that this is the Kerala syllabus, one-to-one, at her chosen times | A hero about the mission rather than the offer |
| Finds her child's class | Classes 1 to 12 tappable from the home page | Burying the classes behind a menu |
| Checks teachers, fees, reviews | Named teachers, a fee basis she can follow, reviews from her own country | Anonymous teachers, no prices, undated praise |
| Taps WhatsApp | Open a chat pre-filled with the class and language she was reading about | A bare "Hi", which costs two messages to recover |
| Everything after | Nothing. It is all human work | No reply, or a reply after two days |

### The pre-filled message is the whole handover

With no form, the pre-filled WhatsApp text is the only way the site can pass what it knows. Treat it as a designed field, not an afterthought. One per page, naming the context:

| Page | Pre-filled message |
| --- | --- |
| Home | `Hi Zaaf, I would like to know more about one-to-one madrassa tuition.` |
| Class page | `Hi Zaaf, I am asking about Class 5 classes for my child. We follow the SKIMVB syllabus.` |
| Syllabus page | `Hi Zaaf, we follow the SKSVB syllabus and I would like to know more.` |
| Exam page | `Hi Zaaf, my child has the Class 10 exam coming up and needs help.` |
| Teacher profile | `Hi Zaaf, I would like to ask about classes with [teacher name].` |
| Fees | `Hi Zaaf, I would like to know the fee for three days a week in Malayalam.` |
| Reviews | `Hi Zaaf, I read the reviews and would like to know more.` |
| Become a Teacher | `Hi Zaaf, I would like to apply to teach.` |

**Track** every one of these as a separate outbound event, labelled by page. That single set of numbers tells Zaaf which page does the persuading, and it is all the site can measure.

### Response, which is the actual product

State reply hours on the site in both Indian and Gulf time, and set a WhatsApp Business greeting and away message so a parent messaging outside hours gets an immediate acknowledgement rather than silence. Name one person responsible. **Promise only what can be met:** a stated four hours that is honoured beats a promised one hour that is not.

Keep a weekly tally by hand — chats started, trials held, students enrolled — because nothing else in this system records it. A spreadsheet with four columns is enough, and without it there is no way to tell a good month from a bad one.

### Flow 2 · Teacher application

Same mechanism, different number if Zaaf prefers to keep teacher enquiries separate. The page states which classes and languages are needed, what the commitment is, and what qualifications are expected, then hands over to WhatsApp. Filtering happens in the conversation, which is the point of having no form.

### Flow 3 · The returning parent

An existing family looking up a timing, a fee or a teacher's name. She should not have to message to find a basic fact. Keep fees, timings and the contact number readable on the site without a conversation; it reduces the load on whoever answers WhatsApp.

### Unhappy paths to design, not discover

- **WhatsApp not installed,** on a laptop or a shared desktop. The tap fails silently. Always show the number as selectable text beside the button, and offer a phone number and email too.
- **Wrong country code.** Write the number in full international form everywhere, including the footer.
- **Messaging outside working hours,** which is normal across a time difference. An away message is not optional here.
- **A class page with no syllabus detail yet.** Write a proper short holding message naming when it will be ready, rather than leaving a thin page.
- **Slow connections.** The site must be usable on a mid-range phone on mobile data, with no large hero image blocking the first screen.
- **An enquiry in Malayalam** when whoever is on duty expects English. Decide who handles which language before launch.

## 9. Voice and messaging

Write to a parent who already knows what a madrassa is and is worried her child is missing one. She needs no persuading that this matters; she needs to know it will work around her week. Practical, warm, and free of guilt.

### Tone

**Practical · warm · respectful · unhurried.**

What it is not: not guilt-driven, not grand, not salesy. Two traps specific to this market:

- **Do not sell on parental guilt.** "Do not let your child grow up without dīn" is coercion, and plenty of competitors use it. A Gulf parent who has moved abroad for work does not need reminding of what her child is missing. She needs to be shown that it is fixable.
- **Do not borrow institutional grandeur.** Zaaf is a small academy with real teachers. Saying that plainly is more convincing than language implying a large institution.

### Key messages

In priority order. The home page carries the first three.

1. **We support your child's madrassa. We do not replace it.** The first thing the site should say, and the reason the sale is easy.
2. **You choose the days, the timings and the language.** The parent is in control. This is the differentiator.
3. **One teacher, one child.** Not a group class on a screen.
4. **Women-led, taught by women.** Girls and sisters at every class, boys up to Class 4. State the boundary rather than leaving it to be discovered.
5. **Exam year? We prepare for Classes 5, 7, 10 and 12.** The most urgent message on the site, for the parent who needs it.

### Vocabulary

| Prefer | Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class 1, Class 5 | Grade, Level, Year | The word Kerala families use, and the structure the site is built on |
| Madrassa | Islamic school, religious academy | Say the thing. The audience knows it |
| Teacher, or her name | Tutor, instructor | This is a teaching relationship, not a gig |
| Fees | Pricing | The word a madrassa family uses |
| One-to-one | Personalised, bespoke | Plain and literal |
| Message us on WhatsApp | Get in touch, enquire now | Says exactly what will happen |
| Timings | Schedule, slots | Indian and Gulf English usage, and what the audience says |

Settle the spelling of **madrassa** and of every Arabic term once, and apply it across every page. Inconsistent spelling is the detail this audience notices first. Decide, too, how teachers are addressed throughout — by name, or with the honorific Zaaf uses in practice — and keep it uniform.

### Language plan

English first. The parents are in the Gulf, often reading on a phone between work, and English is the common medium; many of their children are schooled in it. A **Malayalam version is a strong P1**, not an afterthought: for an older parent, or a grandparent making the case, reading the class descriptions in Malayalam is what makes it feel like home. That means the type system must carry Malayalam script properly from the start, even if the translation arrives later. Do not machine-translate religious content.

Note that the language question appears twice and means different things: the language the **site** is written in, and the language a **class** is taught in. Keep them visibly separate, or parents will assume an English site means English-only classes.

### Positioning options

- **"Madrassa, at your timings."** Leads with the flexibility, which is the real differentiator and the thing no organised madrassa can offer.
- **"The same syllabus, wherever you are."** Leads with continuity, and speaks directly to the parent who left Kerala.
- **"One teacher. Your timetable."** Leads with the format. Clean, but says less about who it is for.

Recommendation: the first, with the second as a supporting line on the home page and the Classes pages. Flexibility is what Zaaf has that the alternatives do not.

## 10. Design-direction brief

Input for whoever designs, not design decisions. No palette, typefaces or layouts are fixed here on purpose.

### Mood

**Orderly · warm · practical · homely · unhurried.**

Orderly comes first and is unusual for this sector. The Kerala madrassa system is itself orderly — numbered classes, a set syllabus, exams at known points — and a design that feels structured and calm is making a claim about the teaching. The competitors look cluttered. Looking organised is a competitive argument here, not just taste.

### The feeling a parent should leave with

*"This is the proper thing, and it will fit into our week."*

Both halves matter. Proper, meaning the real syllabus rather than an improvised substitute. Fits, meaning it bends around a Gulf working week rather than demanding she bend around it.

### What the design must accommodate

| Constraint | Consequence for design |
| --- | --- |
| The Classes 1 to 12 grid is the main navigation device | It appears on the home page and the Classes page and must work at phone width. Twelve tappable targets in a small space is a real layout problem and should be solved early, not late |
| Every page ends in a WhatsApp button, and a fixed mobile bar carries one | Design the button once, properly, and reserve the bar's space from the start. It is the most-used element on the site |
| WhatsApp's own green is strongly recognised | Using it makes the button instantly legible but constrains the palette around it. Decide deliberately: adopt it for that one control, or use the brand colour and rely on the icon |
| Three scripts: Latin, Malayalam, Arabic | The type system must carry all three without looking improvised, even though Malayalam pages come later. Arabic needs a proper face with correct vowel marks |
| No forms anywhere | The usual rhythm of a site like this — hero, content, form — does not apply. Every page resolves to a conversation instead |
| Teachers may have no photograph | The no-portrait state is designed first; the avatar must look intentional beside a real portrait |
| Timings always carry a timezone | Design a consistent way to show two zones together, since every time on the site needs it |
| Mid-range Android, mobile data, often a shared family phone | Weight is a design constraint. Heavy heroes cost conversions here |

### Accessibility target

**WCAG 2.2 AA.** In particular: contrast that survives a phone screen in Gulf daylight, touch targets of at least 44px — which the twelve-class grid will test — visible keyboard focus, and a text equivalent beside each diagram.

### Device priority

1. Phone, portrait, mid-range Android. The majority case; design here first.
2. Phone, portrait, iPhone.
3. Laptop. Mostly teachers, and some parents at work. Note that WhatsApp taps often fail here, which is why the number must also be readable text.
4. Tablet, covered by the responsive range.

### References still needed

The cheapest useful thing you can send: **two links, one site you like and one you dislike, with a line on why each.** They need not be education sites. A clothing shop or a bank reveals taste better than a competitor does.

What the peer scan offers is mostly a list of what to avoid: stock photography, badge clutter, gradients and claims of round-the-clock availability. Zaaf's argument is specificity, so the design should be free to look nothing like that market.

### Existing brand assets

`[CONTENT NEEDED: logo files, any existing colours or typefaces, Instagram or printed material to match — owner: Founder]`. If none exist, a small identity piece joins the critical path before page design, and should include how the name is set in Malayalam as well as Latin script.

## 11. Requirements

Storing nothing makes this a small, cheap and fast project. There is no backend, no database of visitors, no payment integration, no account system and no scheduling tool. What remains is a content site that has to load quickly and hand over cleanly to WhatsApp.

### Functional

| Requirement | Tier | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CMS-managed classes, teachers, reviews, fees and FAQs | P0 | A non-technical person must add a teacher or change a fee without a developer |
| WhatsApp links with per-page pre-filled text | P0 | The site's only handover mechanism. Specified page by page in section 8 |
| Fixed mobile action bar | P0 | WhatsApp and call, always visible |
| Number shown as selectable text | P0 | For laptops, where the WhatsApp link often fails silently |
| Classes 1 to 12 grid | P0 | The main navigation device, and a genuine layout problem at phone width |
| Timings shown with timezone | P0 | Indian and Gulf time together wherever an hour appears |
| Outbound click tracking | P0 | Every WhatsApp tap as an event labelled by page. The only conversion data that exists |
| Exam-preparation page | P1 | Conditional on Zaaf preparing students for the board exams |
| Malayalam locale | P1 | A full second language version, not a translate widget |

### Integrations

| Service | Purpose | Decision needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WhatsApp Business | The entire funnel | The number, who holds the account, greeting and away messages, stated reply hours |
| Analytics | Outbound-tap measurement | Pick one. With no personal data collected, prefer a provider that needs no cookie banner |
| Google Business Profile | Local and brand search | Worth claiming even as an online academy |
| Instagram | Where this audience already is, and a likely traffic source | Link it, and make sure the profile link points at the site |

There is deliberately no payment gateway, no CRM and no email service. Payment is agreed and taken outside the website, which removes the longest-lead and most expensive part of a project like this.

### Non-functional

- **Performance.** Largest contentful paint under 2.5 seconds on a mid-range Android over mobile data. With no forms or application logic, there is no excuse for a slow site here.
- **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.2 AA, tested on real pages.
- **Security.** HTTPS, a plan for platform updates, automated backups. The attack surface is small because nothing is stored, which is a real benefit worth keeping.
- **Privacy.** The site collects nothing. Say so plainly on the privacy page, name the analytics used, and note that WhatsApp conversations are governed by WhatsApp's own terms. Since many visitors are messaging about children, keep it genuinely minimal rather than nominally minimal.
- **Maintainability.** One non-technical person must be able to edit classes, teachers, fees and reviews, or the site is stale within a year.

### SEO groundwork

This site's organic opportunity is unusually good, because the queries are specific and nobody serves them well.

1. **Board queries**, the sharpest of all: SKIMVB syllabus class 7, SKSVB madrassa exam, and the boards' full names. High intent, almost no competition, and served by the two syllabus pages and the exam page.
2. **Class-level queries**, one page each: Class 5 madrassa syllabus online, Class 10 madrassa exam, through all twelve. The main prize, and the reason the class pages exist.
3. **Situation queries:** online madrassa tuition for children in UAE, Malayalam madrassa class online, madrassa tuition for Gulf children.
4. **Malayalam-language queries,** once the Malayalam version exists. Almost nobody is competing there.

At launch: one title and description per page as drafted in section 5; `Organization`, `Course` and `FAQPage` structured data; an XML sitemap and `robots.txt`; a claimed Google Business Profile; and `hreflang` planned for when Malayalam arrives. No redirect map is needed, since nothing is being replaced.

### Legal pages

A privacy notice covering analytics and the WhatsApp handover, and short terms covering what Zaaf provides. No payment, refund or account terms are needed on the site, since none of that happens there — though Zaaf should still have its fee, cancellation and missed-class rules written down somewhere a parent can be sent, because they will be asked for on WhatsApp constantly.

**One point needs a decision before launch, and it is the only real legal risk on this site.** Zaaf's offer is defined by two other organisations' syllabi, and the site will name both boards throughout — which is exactly what makes it findable. If Zaaf is independent of SKIMVB and SKSVB, say so plainly and visibly: a line in the footer and on the About page along the lines of *Zaaf Academy is an independent tuition service and is not affiliated with or endorsed by SKIMVB or SKSVB*. Do not use either board's logo, crest or any wording implying official standing or recognition. Referring factually to a syllabus a child studies is ordinarily fine; implying endorsement is not. If Zaaf is in fact recognised by either board, say that instead and keep the evidence. **Take local advice if there is any doubt**, because this single point deserves more care than everything else on the legal list combined.

### Platform recommendation

**Recommended: a mainstream content-managed platform with the five content types modelled properly.** The deciding factor is not technical, it is that Kerala has a deep and affordable pool of developers who can take this over, and Zaaf must never be stuck waiting on one person to change a fee.

**Alternative: a statically generated site with a hosted CMS.** Faster, cheaper to host and more secure, which suits a site that stores nothing. The pool of people able to maintain it locally is smaller, so choose it only if Zaaf has someone specific in mind.

**Not recommended: a custom application, or any platform that charges per month in foreign currency** for features this site does not use. There are no forms, no logins and no payments to justify it.

## Addendum · Nov 21, 2026 — the personalised course becomes the main category

This document was written when Zaaf was one thing: tuition that supports a child
through her madrassa syllabus. It is now two, and the larger of them is not in
the sections above.

**The personalised course.** Any woman, at any age, can join Zaaf without a
madrassa, without previous study and without an entrance test. She chooses what
she wants to learn — Qur'an reading and Tajweed, Hifz, Tafsir, Hadith, Fiqh,
Aqeedah, Arabic, Seerah and Islamic history, duas and daily practice — and then
agrees a syllabus, a pace and a timetable **with a mentor**, who teaches the
sessions accordingly. Girls who never joined a madrassa and women returning to
their learning after many years are the centre of this, not an edge case.
